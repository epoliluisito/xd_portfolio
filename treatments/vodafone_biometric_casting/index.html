Casting · AI Character Sheets

Shooting reference for biometric casting

How to photograph a performer so their likeness can be turned into an accurate, reusable AI character sheet. The rule is simple: neutral lighting, and enough images to fully describe the face and the body. The character is only ever as good as the reference set.

What we're capturing

Biometric casting, in one line

We capture an individual's facial and body characteristics as reference imagery, and use it to generate a controllable AI likeness of that specific person. Because we are recording a real person's biometric data, every shoot runs on explicit, written consent and a defined usage scope. Coverage and consistency at the shoot are what determine how accurate and stable the final character will be.

The shoot — requirements

Shoot to these five rules

1

Lighting & background

Soft, even, neutral daylight (~5000–5600K). Flat and shadow-free — no hard key, no coloured light, no gels. Plain mid-grey seamless background. Keep exposure consistent across the whole set.

2

Face coverage

Front, both three-quarters, both full profiles. Level, then slightly up and slightly down. Neutral expression first, then a few natural ones (smile, brows up, mouth open, eyes closed). Eyes to lens; hair pinned back for one full pass; glasses off (then on, if relevant).

3

Body coverage

Full-length front, back, both sides and both three-quarters. Relaxed A-pose, arms a little away from the body, hands open and visible, feet in frame. Plain, form-fitting wardrobe so the true silhouette reads. Never crop — always full head-to-toe.

4

Technical

Sharp, high-resolution, correctly exposed, RAW where possible. 50–85mm-equivalent lens and a consistent subject distance to avoid facial distortion. No motion blur, no beauty retouching, no filters.

5

Volume

The more, the better. Aim for 30–50+ frames per subject spanning every angle and expression. Sparse references drift and generalise; dense, consistent references lock the identity.

✓

Why it matters

The model learns the person from these images alone. Neutral, complete, high-volume input gives a faithful, directable likeness; thin or uneven input gives a generic face that wanders between shots.

Consent & data

A likeness is biometric personal data

A person's face and body are biometric personal data — special-category data under GDPR (Art. 9). Before any shoot, secure a signed model release and consent covering the creation and use of the AI likeness, with a defined scope, duration and territory. Store the reference set and derived models securely, share on a need-to-know basis, and use them only for the agreed purpose.

On the day

Quick checklist

  • Signed consent / likeness release in place
  • Mid-grey seamless, soft even daylight (~5000–5600K)
  • Face: front, ¾s, profiles, up/down tilts, expressions
  • Body: front, back, sides, ¾s, A-pose, full-length
  • 50–85mm, sharp, RAW, no retouch or filters
  • 30–50+ frames per subject, nothing cropped
  • Files named per subject and stored securely
  • One pass hair back / glasses off, then variants
