The constant

What every Plussie shares

A walking, human-height pink plus — rounded body, white cartoon gloves, simple face. He is value-obsessed, a champion of good deals, a little mad, and totally on your side.

  • DNA (from the brief): the kind heart of Barney, the obsession of Cookie Monster, the chaos of Mr Blobby.
  • His reactions to value are the point: weeps at a free month for a referral; throws a party for double data; gets emotional that the price you sign up to is the price you keep.
  • The flip side: a hidden fee is a crime; charging loyal customers more than new ones is a betrayal.
  • World & sign-off: the Plusnet magenta world, and “that’s a plus.”
All of the above is fixed. It does not change between the four looks.
Look 1 of 4

Original — matte felt

The read

Classic, clean, unmistakably a mascot. Flat felt, simple dot eyes. Reads timeless and brand-safe, and stays legible at any size.

What changes vs the others

Least texture of the four; the simplest to animate and keep consistent. Expression comes more from body and posture than from the face.

Best suited to

Broad brand film — the polished 60% “Advertising Plussie”. The safe, premium default.

15–20s animation

A clean single-scene brand beat. E.g. Ab Fab: open tight on Plussie, eyes shut, blissful → pull wide to a star-float in a pool, TV on the side → endline “that’s a plus.” Felt keeps it clean and premium.

Look 2 of 4

Short Fur — “Fluffy” (lead option)

The read

Soft, huggable and by far the most expressive. Pixar-style eyes that can do heart-eyes and full meltdowns. The warmest, most emotional read.

What changes vs the others

Fur catches light and reads tactile and lovable. The face carries big reactions, so it lands the “weeps at value” comedy better than any other finish.

Best suited to

Big-feelings comedy and social.

15–20s animation

An over-reaction beat. E.g. Gordon Ramsay: close on Plussie fixed on his phone, hand shaking → pull wide, he’s frantically cooking a BBQ watching Ramsay → he melts down → endline. Fur maximises the funny.

Look 3 of 4

Knit Wool — crochet

The read

Handmade, crafted, charming. Chunky wool reads warm, human and “made with care” — closest to a real costume mascot in a crowd.

What changes vs the others

Lower-fi and folksy by design. The texture feels authentic and community-made, which is a strength for IRL and user-style social rather than polish.

Best suited to

Social mascot and community moments — the 10% spontaneous cut. Strong for referral / “bring a mate”.

15–20s animation

A social / IRL vignette. E.g. a street-party or festival referral: Plussie rallying mates — “bring a friend, we’ll pay both your bills”. The knit sells the real-mascot, in-the-crowd feel.

Look 4 of 4

3D Render / Chrome — CGI

The read

Premium, slick, modern. Polished CGI (or reflective chrome) reads expensive and product-forward.

What changes vs the others

The most “designed” and least handmade. High-gloss surfaces make products and prices feel like a hero — but carries less warmth than fur or knit.

Best suited to

Conversion and offers — the 30% functional “Conversion Plussie”, and DRTV.

15–20s animation

A product / offer hero. E.g. Black Friday: Plussie sips a cocktail → pull wide to him on a motorised pool lilo surrounded by Black Friday loot, router + price supered. The gloss sells the deal.

Character → animation

How to translate it into 15–20 seconds

One continuous scene, no cross-cutting. The character shows through the reaction, not the edit. Scale the expression to the look — fur biggest, felt cleanest.

1
Misdirect

Tight on Plussie’s face; an expression that reads one way.

2
Reveal

Camera pulls wide to show the value situation.

3
Payoff

A big Plussie reaction — the funny.

4
Product beat

Router / price in the pink world.

5
Endline

“That’s a plus.”

System1 rules baked in: make it funny, lean on the show property, one scene unfolding. Worked examples: Black Friday Lilo, Gordon Ramsay, Ab Fab.
