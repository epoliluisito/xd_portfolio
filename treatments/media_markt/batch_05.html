Posting copy

It's Black November Deal Maxxing! Time to maxx your view with our best TV deals at mediamarkt.com!

Input production

“

For us, the humor is mainly in the casting of the employee. He should be a very unoptimized guy who genuinely doesn't care about looksmaxxing at all. More of a laid-back, indulgent type, super confident, kind of Zach Galifianakis.

How to read it. The joke is a pun with a face attached: ‘looksmaxxing’ is about self-optimisation, and he is optimising a television instead. It only works if the man is visibly, cheerfully uninterested in optimising himself — the casting is the punchline.
01 · Casting

Who is in this picture.

A still has one moment — the casting has to do all the work at once.

One character: the employee. Male, 30s–40s. Soft, unbothered, entirely at ease with himself — the brief's Zach Galifianakis reference is exactly right. Beard, a bit shaggy, comfortable in his own skin. Super confident, not sheepish. He is not the butt of the joke; he is the one person in the picture who has nothing to prove, and the picture is funnier for it.

What the frame needs

  • Not groomed, not styled, not gym-cut. Real hair, real posture, real body.
  • The expression is proud presenter — an open-palm game-show gesture toward the screen.
  • European, not identifiably from one country. MediaMarkt polo, worn as a man who does not fuss with clothes.
  • Reference mismatch: the visual in the brief shows a tidy, conventionally groomed employee. That is not the note. Recast toward the description.
01 · Setting

Where it happens.

Built from reference, never hallucination.

A MediaMarkt TV wall. Bright store lighting, a large screen filling most of the frame with a high-contrast landscape on it — the picture quality has to sell itself at thumbnail size.

What it has to hold

  • Big TV as the hero, angled so the screen content reads clearly at small scale.
  • Store depth behind — shelving, other screens, real shop.
  • Half logo only on any signage, per the campaign rule.
  • Screen content is a post comp; generate the display neutral.
01 · The frame

Composition and prompt.

All type, supers and the Deal Maxxing lockup are composited in post — generate the picture only.
Composition. Vertical card. He stands to one side, screen to the other — a clean two-part composition that survives the crop and leaves headroom for the super and the Deal Maxxing lockup.
The frame

Photoreal vertical image of a relaxed, unstyled European man in his late 30s with a beard and a soft build, wearing a red retail polo shirt, standing beside a large flat-screen television on a bright electronics shop floor. He gestures toward the screen with an open palm, proud and completely at ease, smiling at camera. Shop shelving and other screens softly behind. Even bright store lighting. Screen content composited in post — generate the display as a plain neutral panel. No text, no logos, no watermark. Naturalistic and candid, real skin and posture, not groomed or glamorous. 9:16 safe centre.

Watch. Cast this one first and show options — the whole meme lives or dies on the face, and the reference visual is currently pulling the wrong way.

She should be enjoying her lunch break; it shouldn't feel too much like efficiency maxxing. Please use the own brand products we used for the moving images.

How to read it. The tone note is the whole brief here. Played as productivity, this becomes bleak — a woman eating at her desk because she has no time. Played as pleasure, it becomes aspirational: she has built herself a proper lunch without leaving the room. She is having a nice time.
02 · Casting

Who is in this picture.

A still has one moment — the casting has to do all the work at once.

One character. Woman, late 20s to 30s, working from home. Relaxed posture — leaning back, feet up or legs crossed, mid-bite and enjoying it. Not hunched, not rushing, not looking at the screen while she eats.

What the frame needs

  • The expression is contentment, eyes closed or half-closed on the bite if it helps.
  • Body language does the work: away from the laptop, not toward it.
  • Home clothes, comfortable. European, not identifiably from one country.
  • No hint of stress, clock-watching or multitasking.
02 · Setting

Where it happens.

Built from reference, never hallucination.

A home desk that has become a kitchen. Warm daylight, plants, a real room — the comedy is that the appliances have migrated onto the work surface and nobody sees a problem with it.

What it has to hold

  • Desk with laptop, plus the own-brand appliances used in the films — KOENIC air fryer and blender.
  • Continuity: match the exact SKUs from Batch 3/4 so the campaign reads as one world.
  • Domestic and lived in — not a styled home-office set.
  • Warm natural window light.
02 · The frame

Composition and prompt.

All type, supers and the Deal Maxxing lockup are composited in post — generate the picture only.
Composition. Vertical, side-on. The desk reads left to right — appliances, laptop, then her leaning back with the food — so the gag is legible in one pass.
The frame

Photoreal vertical image of a relaxed European woman in her late 20s working from home, sitting side-on at a wooden desk, leaning back away from her laptop, mid-bite into a toasted sandwich and visibly enjoying it. On the same desk beside the laptop: a KOENIC air fryer and a KOENIC blender, in use. Warm natural daylight from a window, plants, a real lived-in room. Comfortable home clothes. Products rendered accurately. No text, no logos, no watermark. Naturalistic, candid, unhurried. 9:16 safe centre.

Watch. Two failure modes: it reads as sad desk-lunch, or it reads as a kitchen-appliance ad. The fix for both is the same — her posture and her face. Leaning back and enjoying it, every time.

Input production

“

The facial expression should contrast between the top: “satisfied and tired” and the bottom: “tired but happy and full of adrenaline.” The setting and locations are already pretty on point in the prompt.

How to read it. The only two-panel meme in the batch, and the only one where the format itself carries the joke. Both panels are the same person, the same night, the same tiredness — what changes is what she is tired for. Everything rests on the two expressions being cousins, not opposites.
03 · Casting

Who is in this picture.

A still has one moment — the casting has to do all the work at once.

One character, two panels. Woman, 20s–30s. She must be unmistakably the same person in both frames, which makes this the one meme with a genuine identity-consistency problem.

What the frame needs

  • Top — satisfied and tired: soft, settled, winding down. Eyelids heavy, content.
  • Bottom — tired but happy, full of adrenaline: the same tiredness, now lit from inside. Eyes wide, grinning.
  • Same hair, same face, ideally the same top — the shift is state, not styling.
  • Technical: lock her sheet and generate both panels from the same reference set.
03 · Setting

Where it happens.

Built from reference, never hallucination.

Two rooms, two colour temperatures. Top: a bed, warm and dim, red ambient lamp glow. Bottom: a gaming desk, cold blue and magenta RGB. The palettes should feel like two halves of one night.

What it has to hold

  • Top: bedroom, low warm/red ambient light, phone in hand, settled under the covers.
  • Bottom: gaming setup, RGB keyboard and lights, monitor glow, controller in hand.
  • Both panels horizontal crops stacked in the card — compose for a letterbox, not a full vertical.
  • Screen and monitor content composited in post.
03 · The frame

Composition and prompt.

All type, supers and the Deal Maxxing lockup are composited in post — generate the picture only.
Composition. Two stacked panels with the supers between and above. Each panel is wide and shallow, so keep faces centred and close — there is very little vertical room per frame.
Panel 1 · Expectation

Photoreal image of a European woman in her late 20s lying in bed at night, propped on a pillow, holding a phone. Satisfied and tired — heavy eyelids, soft content half-smile, winding down. Warm dim bedroom, a red ambient lamp glowing behind. Screen content composited in post. Naturalistic and candid, real skin. Wide, shallow horizontal crop with her face centred. No text, no logos.

Panel 2 · Reality

Photoreal image of the same woman at a gaming desk later that night, controller in both hands, leaning toward a monitor. Tired but happy and full of adrenaline — same tiredness as panel 1, now wide-eyed and grinning. Cold blue and magenta RGB lighting, keyboard glow, monitor light on her face. Screen content composited in post. Wide, shallow horizontal crop, face centred. No text, no logos.

Watch. The expressions are the deliverable. If the top reads miserable or the bottom reads manic, the joke inverts. Generate both panels together and judge them as a pair, never separately.

Input production

“

The setup of this life hack should feel as realistic as possible. Maybe we could have a glimpse of a couch with a leg hanging down, or the leg of a desk? Maybe that's too much. Let's try it out and see.

How to read it. The only meme with no face in it — the whole gag is a piece of evidence. A mouse tied to a robot vacuum, dragged in slow circles so the status light never goes amber. It has to read as a real hack someone actually rigged, not a product shot.
04 · Casting

Who is in this picture.

A still has one moment — the casting has to do all the work at once.

No character in frame — and that is the risk. Without a human trace this becomes a photograph of a robot vacuum, and the joke evaporates.

What the frame needs

  • Our recommendation: take the glimpse. A bare leg hanging off a sofa at the edge of frame is enough to place a person in the room without showing one.
  • It answers ‘whose hack is this?’ in a fraction of a second — and it is what makes it a meme rather than a still life.
  • Keep it to one human element. A whole sleeping man in frame is a different, weaker picture.
  • If it proves too much in testing, the fallback is a worn slipper or a dropped hoodie instead of the leg.
04 · Setting

Where it happens.

Built from reference, never hallucination.

An ordinary living room floor. Real wood, real dust, real daylight. The less styled this is, the more it reads as documentation of something that genuinely happened.

What it has to hold

  • Wooden floor, low camera, shallow domestic depth.
  • Generic robot vacuum — no visible brand.
  • A wired mouse on a taut string, tied to the vacuum, visibly being towed.
  • The string must read as improvised — knotted, slightly too long, not neat.
04 · The frame

Composition and prompt.

All type, supers and the Deal Maxxing lockup are composited in post — generate the picture only.
Composition. Low and close on the floor, vacuum and mouse in the same plane, with the sofa leg or a foot just entering the top or edge of frame.
The frame

Photoreal vertical image, low camera close to a wooden living-room floor. A generic black robot vacuum slowly towing a wired computer mouse behind it on a knotted length of string, the string taut. Ordinary lived-in room, natural daylight, real dust and floor texture. At the edge of frame, a bare human leg hangs off the end of a sofa — just a glimpse, no face, no body. Documentary and unstyled, as if photographed on a phone. No brand marks, no text, no logos, no watermark. 9:16 safe centre.

Watch. Two versions worth generating: with the leg and without. The brief is undecided, and this is cheaper to answer with two frames than with a conversation.

Pssst … it's Black November Deal Maxxing! Time to maxx your life with our best deals at mediamarkt.com!

Input production

“

The most important thing is the contrast between the man and his surroundings. Kids screaming, football fans, business calls… the man in focus doesn't notice any of it. Maybe his mouth is slightly open and he's even snoring? We can definitely play around with this and see what makes the idea come across in the funniest way.

How to read it. A one-frame contrast gag: maximum noise, total serenity, in the same picture. The headphones are the punchline but the composition is the joke — chaos has to be visible around him at a glance.
05 · Casting

Who is in this picture.

A still has one moment — the casting has to do all the work at once.

One hero plus a crowd. Man, 30s–40s, headphones on, completely gone. Around him: a screaming child, football fans mid-celebration, someone shouting into a phone.

What the frame needs

  • The hero does nothing. Head back, eyes shut, mouth slightly open — genuinely asleep, not posing as calm.
  • Yes to the snore. The slack jaw is what makes it funny rather than merely peaceful — it says he is beyond reach.
  • The crowd must be mid-action, not just present: mouths open, arms up, phones raised.
  • Everyone European-mixed, ordinary, no caricature.
05 · Setting

Where it happens.

Built from reference, never hallucination.

A packed aeroplane cabin — the most legible confined chaos there is, and it stacks people at different depths naturally.

What it has to hold

  • Narrow cabin, seats front and back, overhead lockers.
  • Chaos at three depths: beside him, behind him, across the aisle.
  • ISY ANC headphones — own brand, must read accurately.
  • Flat cabin lighting; no drama in the light, all the drama in the people.
05 · The frame

Composition and prompt.

All type, supers and the Deal Maxxing lockup are composited in post — generate the picture only.
Composition. He sits centre, calm and still, with movement filling every edge. The vertical crop is tight, so the chaos has to be stacked in depth rather than spread sideways.
The frame

Photoreal vertical image inside a packed aeroplane cabin. Centre frame: a European man in his late 30s asleep in his seat wearing ISY over-ear noise-cancelling headphones, head back, eyes closed, mouth slightly open, completely serene. All around him at different depths: a screaming toddler, football fans in team colours mid-celebration with arms raised, a passenger shouting into a phone. Everyone else is loud and moving; he is perfectly still. Flat cabin lighting. Headphones rendered accurately. Naturalistic and candid, no caricature. No text, no logos, no watermark. 9:16 safe centre.

Watch. The chaos must be legible at thumbnail. If it reads as a generic busy plane, the contrast dies — each disturbance needs a clear silhouette and its own pocket of the frame.
