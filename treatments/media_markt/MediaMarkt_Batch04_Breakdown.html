The brief, as written

A man is preparing a romantic dinner for two. Romantic smart light atmosphere. Romantic music from the boom box. Cooking with the airfryer, the blender, the contact grill.

01 · Casting

Who has to be in this film.

One character — the personality first, the face second.
Character 1
The host of a party for one

He is not sad and not a joke to himself. He has taken himself out, properly, and he is having a lovely time. Play him sincere and faintly smug — the comedy comes from how seriously he has committed to the romance, not from pity.

Appearance

European, not identifiably from one country. Early-to-mid 30s. Dark, slightly unruly wavy hair, light stubble, warm mid-tone skin. Highly expressive brows and eyes — the entire payoff is a face mid-double-bite. Softly built, real, never gym-cut or model-handsome.

Outfit

Dressed for the date: a good shirt, sleeves pushed up, a fine knit over it. Home-smart, not going-out smart. Socks, no shoes. The effort is the joke — he made it for nobody.

Expressions the sheet must carry

Pleased anticipation · quiet pride · mid-bite bliss (both hands full) · a small private toast

One location, and it has to carry the whole gag before a word is spoken: an open kitchen that has been dressed for a date. The room must read as somebody's real home first and a romantic set second — the smart lights are doing theatre on top of an ordinary Tuesday.

The brief exactly as delivered — nothing added, nothing interpreted.

The brief, as written

Monday on a completely packed subway. Everyone is squeezed tightly together, practically crammed into the carriage. Everyone is extremely annoyed. We enjoy some of these Monday faces.

05 · Location

Where this story lives.

Hero image first — one frame that represents the location and is right for the narrative.

Why this location

One location, used twice — and that is the whole technical problem of this film. A designed open-plan kitchen-living room in green and anthracite, seen in daylight and again in the evening, with exactly one object changed. If the room drifts between the two halves, the joke evaporates.

What it has to contain

  • Open-plan kitchen and living room, deliberately designed — matched cabinetry, considered objects, nothing accidental.
  • Palette locked to deep green and anthracite, warm wood and stone as neutrals. Restrained and adult.
  • It should still feel lived in rather than a showroom — a book left out, a used cup, real texture.
  • A clear appliance bay in the kitchen run where a washing machine sits fully visible in wide shot. That bay is the stage.
  • Must hold in two lighting states: flat daylight, and warm low evening light.
