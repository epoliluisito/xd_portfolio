Father’s Day · Film Treatment 2026

NIVEANESS

We never outgrow care.

Care changes shape across a lifetime.

It begins as something physical:

a bandage, a hand, sunscreen on small shoulders.

Later, it becomes quieter. Less visible. More restrained.

But for a father, one thing never changes…

… you never stop being his baby.

Why NIVEA owns this story

A brand that has always been where care happens.

A century of care

For over a century, NIVEA has been part of the vocabulary of everyday care — not in boardrooms or on runways, but in bathrooms, handbags and kitchen tables. In the small, private moments between people who love each other.

No other brand lives so naturally in the gestures this film is built from: the hand reaching for the cream, the tin on the shelf, the sunscreen slipped into a bag.

This is not a great film that features NIVEA.

This is a great NIVEA film.

The Idea

A father waits. Memory takes over.

The film uses a hospital waiting room as its emotional engine.

Placed in a state of total helplessness — the one situation where his care cannot act — we open the door to everything he has ever felt.

The reversal reveals something deeper: while everyone rushes to the newborn, he walks straight to his daughter.

That gesture does not just surprise — it names the film’s entire subject.

To him, she is still the one who needs to be checked on first.

Tone

Tender. Precise. Earned.

Not sentimental. Not performative.

The emotion comes from recognition — small gestures, real friction, lifelong love.

The final line must feel discovered — not written.

Storyline

A man waits in a hospital corridor.

As he sits alone with his fear, we move through fragments…

Her birth · Her first fall · The costume made at night · The cream on his face

The sunscreen in the bag · The boyfriend · The silence · The wedding aisle

Then the doors open. He walks straight to her.

Characters

The Father

Late sixties. Warm. Weathered. Slightly awkward — a man whose love comes out through action, not language. He should feel real, not idealised. His eyes must carry decades. A father whose love is embarrassing and total.

We see him at two life stages — the same hands, the same instinct to reach.

The strongest casting test: you must see the young father in the old man.

Characters

The Daughter

She must feel specific, not symbolic. Across the years we see her as child, teenager, young woman, new mother — joyful, furious, embarrassed, independent, tender. That full range, including the friction, is what makes the ending land.

At every age, the child is still there — in the eyes, the laugh, the way she tilts her head. Continuity of self across the decades, not just continuity of plot.

Acting

Care expressed through action.

Reaching for a bandage. Making a costume at night.

Letting her cover his face with cream. Holding back on the wedding aisle.

The daughter’s performance must carry the full register of growing up. Her friction is not a flaw — it is what makes his love feel real.

The final hospital scene: almost documentary.

No performance. Just presence.

Settings

The Hospital

A maternity ward corridor in a mid-sized public hospital. Not glamorous, not grim — functional. Long fluorescent-lit hallways, pale walls, linoleum floors. He is the only person sitting still. The space is indifferent to his fear.

Settings

Spaces of Memory

The family home — a lived-in kitchen, morning light through a condensation-fogged window. A bathroom shelf with products lined up. Her childhood bedroom, changing across the years: drawings giving way to band posters, then bare paint, then empty.

A hospital delivery room — thirty years earlier. His young hands. A tiny fist.

A primary school hall — rows of parents in the dark, stage light ahead.

A church aisle — white flowers on dark wood pews, his buttonhole slightly crooked. A dinner table, the same across twenty years. A ski slope…

These spaces are not reconstructed. They are remembered — slightly imperfect, slightly warm, slightly more beautiful than they were.

Time Itself

The film spans roughly 35 years. Chronology is never explained — it is felt through texture, clothing and the ageing of faces. No title cards, no dates. The audience always knows roughly where they are; they never need to be told.

A strong temporal anchor: his hands.

Young and quick in the memories. Folded and still in the corridor.

Location Scouting

Defined by function and era — not by country.

A pan-European world: recognisable, lived-in, warm without being specific. The more universal the world, the more universal the story.

The Script

The essential idea, given a dramatic structure.

The developed version builds structure around the original idea — so every scene earns its place, and the ending arrives not as a surprise, but as an inevitability.

What we removed, and why: the foot-tapping opening (replaced with his arrival), the shopping mall, the social-network scene, the “be careful” texts, the dishes argument, the SPF on the ski slope.

Each cut trades the illustrative for the structural — shown, not told.

Key developments: a structured opening where the hands bridge into memory; a memory arc that moves care from visible to invisible; a camera grammar shifting from his perspective to hers; transitions that connect rather than cut; the brand present across every era — never announced, always there.

Cinematography

Curated. Precise. In service of the story.

The present-day hospital is shot with control — longer lenses, shallow depth, he is in focus, the world is not. Light is practical and cold, favouring no one.

In the memories, light sources are always visible — a lamp, a window, a school stage. You always know where the warmth is coming from.

It should feel like a film that happens to be an advertisement.

Not an advertisement that has borrowed the grammar of film.

Formats & the Brand Across Time

From 16mm to mobile. NIVEA was always there.

Mixing formats is not a stylistic choice — it is an argument. Each era has its own texture, and NIVEA is in all of them. Not as a placement. As a constant.

Quietly, a brand archive — a product present in every version of this family’s life, across more than a century of moments that looked exactly like this one. The film does not say this. It shows it.

Sound & Music

Structure, not atmosphere.

Music enters quietly, grows, then disappears before the final walk. The last line plays in near-silence — just his voice, just the room.

The hospital hum, the camcorder whir, the skiing clip tinny from a phone speaker — each sound world belongs to its era. When the formats collide, so do the sounds. That collision is intentional.

Product Integration

NIVEA does not interrupt. It accumulates.

The tin at the first fall. The cream on his face. The sunscreen in the bag. The tin at the bedside.

By the end, the brand is not making a claim. It is carrying memory.

Emotion leads. The brand gains meaning through association.

Never through interruption.
